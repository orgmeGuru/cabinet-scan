Connect

Every document, connected — the LifeGraph

Your LifeGraph is one connected picture of everything your documents mention — the people, organisations, accounts, policies, vehicles, obligations and dates — merged so that two letters about the same landlord become one landlord, and a premium that changed becomes a flagged change.

The graph is assembled and stored on your device and never leaves it — the analysis service only ever sees one document at a time and never the assembled whole. Explore it as a map, tap any node to jump to the document behind it, and it opens focused on whatever your most recent document changed.

A “Life picture” score shows how complete your records are and names the few documents worth adding next — so it's obvious what the app can do for you, and what it can't yet.

Overview

A dashboard for your actual life

One screen that reads your LifeGraph back to you — current home, employer, vehicles, policies, loans, subscriptions, dependents — with a “This week” card that gathers everything dated in one place: to-dos, renewals, and the notice-period cut-offs where you have to cancel now to avoid another year.

It also totals what you're paying across every contract, keeps your IBANs and policy numbers a tap from copy, and flags contradictions between documents. A “What changed” feed narrates new paperwork in plain words — home insurance €820 → €960, renews 30 November — and the home screen's short “For you” list surfaces the few things that need you, without your having to look.

Privacy

Your data stays on your device. Always.

Every scan, your history, your to-dos and your entire LifeGraph live in the app's own storage on your iPhone — file-protected and left out of device and iCloud backups. None of it is ever uploaded.

Local-first by default

Scanning, text recognition, naming, the key-facts card, filing and the whole LifeGraph run entirely on your device with no network. The app works fully offline.

Nothing is kept, if you opt in

Turn on “AI analysis” and only the recognised text of one document at a time is sent — for a better name, summary and graph fragment. No account, no database; each request is handled on its own and never stored or used to train models. Only brief, content-free load logs are kept. The picture of your life stays on your phone.

No account, no trackers

No sign-in, no advertising, no third-party trackers, no cross-app tracking. Photo Notes is the one feature that sends an image (to be transcribed), and it's off until you turn it on.

You hold the data — and the delete key

Export everything as one zip — every PDF in its folders, the LifeGraph as JSON, a readable summary — and keep your own copy. Or erase everything on the device in a couple of taps: documents, history, LifeGraph, to-dos. Files you synced to iCloud Drive or Google Drive stay where you put them.

FAQ

Questions

Does anything leave my phone?

Not by default. Scanning, OCR, naming, filing and the LifeGraph all run on-device. If you switch on “AI analysis” in Settings, the recognised text of a document — one at a time, never the page images — is sent to the analysis service for a better name, summary and graph fragment. Photo Notes is the exception: it sends the photo you take, and it's off until you enable it.

Does the AI service remember my documents?

No. It has no accounts and no database. Each request is handled on its own and isn't stored, and the text isn't used to train models. The only thing it keeps is a brief, content-free load log — a random per-install ID, which feature ran and how much text — deleted within about 90 days. It processes your documents in isolation, and never sees the merged picture of your life, which is assembled and kept only on your iPhone.

What languages does it handle?

On-device text recognition covers English and German. With AI analysis on, documents in essentially any language are understood, and you can have summaries in English, German, or “match the document”.

What's the “country” setting for?

It tells the analysis and the Ask experts which country's rules, forms, authorities and deadlines apply to you — so a tax or housing question gets the right answer for where you live, not a generic one. It defaults to your device region.

Where are my files stored?

In the app's own folder in the Files app by default — you can browse the whole Area / Subject tree there. Point it at a folder inside iCloud Drive or Google Drive and finished PDFs are copied there automatically, using the system file provider (no extra sign-in in the app).

Can I get my data out?

Yes — “Export everything” in Settings builds a single zip on your device: every PDF in its Area / Subject folders, your LifeGraph as a JSON file, and a plain-text summary of every document and to-do. Save it to Files, AirDrop it, or mail it to yourself.

How do I get it — and is it free?

It's on the App Store: get Cabinet Scan. It is free for the first three months while we're in testing. After that, the optional AI features may move to a paid plan; scanning, naming and filing on your device stay free. No ads, no account — ever.
